Privacy Policy

Cortez iOS App — published by RALL DIGITAL LLC

What Cortez Does

Cortez turns your iPhone or iPad into a Bluetooth keyboard and trackpad for a computer you can see. It uses your device's camera to read the computer's screen on the device itself, and sends keyboard and pointer input to the computer over Bluetooth, the same way a hardware keyboard does.

Data We Collect

We do not collect personal data. The only exception is the limited records described under AI Help with Sign-ins, and only if you turn that feature on. Specifically:

  • No accounts. Cortez does not require sign-up, login, or any account creation.
  • No analytics. The app contains no analytics or advertising SDK. We do not track usage, sessions, taps, or behavior.
  • No advertising. There are no ads and no ad tracking.
  • No data sold. We do not sell, share, or monetize any user data.
  • Nothing leaves your device unless you turn on AI help with sign-ins. Cortez never sends camera frames, screenshots, saved passwords, or contact details to any server. Screen text is sent only by AI help with sign-ins, which is off by default and described below.

Camera

Cortez uses your device's camera to view the computer screen you point it at. Camera frames are processed entirely on your device for text recognition and screen detection, and are discarded after processing. They are not stored and not transmitted.

Bluetooth

Cortez connects to your computer over Bluetooth Low Energy as a standard HID (Human Interface Device) keyboard and pointer. The connection is direct between your iPhone or iPad and your computer; no data passes through our servers. Only keyboard and pointer input reports are transmitted.

Passwords, Face ID and Touch ID

Cortez does not store passwords. When you use Fill password, you choose the password yourself from iOS AutoFill, which is part of iOS and asks for Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode according to your device settings. Cortez receives only the username and password you choose, types them as keystrokes into the computer you are controlling, and never saves them or transmits them anywhere else. Separately, you can lock Cortez itself behind Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode.

Face Data

Cortez does not collect, access, store, or share face data. When you unlock Cortez, iOS performs Face ID or Touch ID itself; Cortez receives only a yes/no result from Apple's LocalAuthentication framework and never sees, stores, or transmits your face geometry, images, or any biometric template. When iOS AutoFill asks for Face ID or Touch ID before giving you a password, that check happens entirely inside iOS. Cortez does not use face detection, face tracking, or the TrueDepth camera. Camera frames of your computer screen are processed on-device and discarded immediately.

Contacts

This version of Cortez does not read your contacts.

Microphone and Speech

This version of Cortez does not use the microphone or speech recognition.

Motion and Local Network

Cortez reads the device's motion sensor on the device only to tell when the device is being held still, so it can read the screen more accurately; motion data is not stored or transmitted. This version of Cortez does not access your local network.

On-Device Storage

Cortez stores the following locally on your iPhone or iPad, in the app's sandbox:

  • Preferences (display name, handedness, platform choice)
  • Saved devices (Bluetooth device names and platform types)
  • Action history (which actions ran and whether they succeeded)

Cortez stores no passwords.

This version does not sync any of this to iCloud. You can erase everything from Settings inside the app ("Erase all Cortez data"). "Erase all Cortez data" also clears anything Cortez has placed in the iOS Keychain (iOS can keep Keychain items after an app is deleted). Deleting the app removes the rest.

AI Help with Sign-ins (optional, off by default)

When Cortez's built-in steps can't finish a sign-in on your computer, it can ask an AI model for the next step. This feature is off by default. Nothing is sent until you tap Allow on the "AI help with sign-ins" sheet, which Cortez shows before it would first send anything. If you tap Not Now, nothing is sent.

What is sent. A text description of the computer screen your camera sees: everything readable on it (which can include names, email addresses, or messages from other apps and notifications that are visible), the names of fields and buttons, and where they are on the screen. It also includes the goal ("Sign in to" the site) and a short list of the steps already tried. Close windows you don't want read before you start.

  • No images. No photo, camera frame, or screenshot is ever sent. The text is read from the camera image on your phone, and the image stays on your phone.
  • Your saved password is never sent. Cortez replaces your saved username and password with placeholders before the text leaves your phone, and types them on your computer itself, over Bluetooth. The AI model can only ask Cortez to "type the password"; it never receives it.
  • Known limits of that replacement. If the camera reads only a piece of a password shorter than six characters, that piece is not recognized and can be sent. The width of a password field on screen can hint at how long a password is. A username shown in a different form (for example, a display name) is not recognized as your username.

Where it goes. The text goes from your phone over an encrypted connection to Cortez's server (which runs on Cloudflare), then to OpenRouter, which passes it only to a provider that keeps no copy of it. Today that provider is Google. Cortez's server refuses any answer served by a provider that is not on that list. If we add a different provider, we will update this policy and ask for your agreement again before anything is sent to it.

What is kept. Cortez's server does not store or log the screen text, the goal, or the steps; it logs only a short status code and how long the request took. Cortez and the provider do not store the text or use it for training. Turning it off. You can turn this off at any time in Settings > Privacy & Data > "AI Help with Sign-ins". When you turn it off, your phone forgets its agreement first, then tells our server, and Cortez stops sending screen text. If we change the wording of what we ask you to agree to, the old agreement stops working and Cortez asks you again.

Third-Party Services

Cortez uses no third-party services and makes no network requests on your behalf unless you turn on AI help with sign-ins. When that feature is on, it uses:

  • Cloudflare, which hosts Cortez's server;
  • OpenRouter, which routes the request to the AI provider;
  • Google (Gemini, served by Google Cloud), the AI provider that reads the screen text and suggests the next step.

No other third party receives data from Cortez. Any new cloud-assisted feature will be added to this policy before it is enabled, and will be off by default.

Children

Cortez is not directed at children. You must be 18 or older to use it. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone.
