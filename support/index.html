What you need

  • An iPhone running iOS 26.2 or later.
  • A computer, TV, or Apple TV that accepts Bluetooth keyboards. Most Macs, Windows PCs, Linux machines, Chromebooks, and Apple TVs do. No software is installed on the computer.
  • A place to prop the phone where its camera can see the screen (a stand or a lean against something works).

Pairing, step by step

  1. Open Cortez and allow Bluetooth and camera access when asked.
  2. On the computer, open its Bluetooth settings and look for a keyboard named Cortez (you can rename it in Cortez settings).
  3. Pair it. If the computer shows a pairing code, accept it on both sides.
  4. Point the phone's camera at the screen. When Cortez recognizes the screen, the keyboard and trackpad become available and the on-screen actions light up.

Troubleshooting

  • The computer does not see Cortez. Make sure Bluetooth is on for the phone, Cortez has Bluetooth permission (iOS Settings → Cortez), and the app is open in the foreground while pairing.
  • It paired once, now it will not connect. On the computer, "forget" or remove the Cortez device, then pair again from step 2. This clears a stale pairing key on either side.
  • Typing lands in the wrong place. Click into the field you want on the computer first, or use the trackpad in Cortez to move focus. Cortez only types where the computer's keyboard focus is.
  • Screen reading is not working. Reduce glare, move closer, and keep the whole screen in frame. Very high-contrast or very small text reads best when the phone is steady.
  • Face ID prompt when filling a password. This is intentional: Cortez asks for Face ID every time before typing a saved password.
  • Start over. Cortez settings → Privacy → "Erase all Cortez data" removes saved devices, passwords, and history from the phone.

Privacy in one line

This version of Cortez sends nothing off your phone. Screen reading happens on the device and keystrokes go to your computer over Bluetooth. Details: Privacy Policy · Terms of Service.
